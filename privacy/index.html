Privacy Policy

Effective: January 2026  |  Last updated: 25 September 2026

Overview

Thurin Labs LLC ("Thurin Labs," "we," "our," or "us") builds Thurin.id (thurin.id), PGP keys on Ethereum addresses. Thurin.id is a lookup site and an attest page (thurin.id/attest) that puts a PGP key on an Ethereum address, with proofs linking it to accounts on other platforms. This policy covers thurin.id, thurinlabs.id, docs.thurin.id, mathom.thurinlabs.id, the keyserver at keys.thurin.id, the relay at relay.thurin.id, and the copies of those sites served under our ENS names.

Thurin.id has no accounts and no database of users. Everything you publish goes on the Ethereum blockchain from your own wallet, and checking an identity runs in your own browser. The only Thurin Labs systems that see any traffic are the web servers and three small services described below: the keyserver, the relay, and the share-card service.

What Goes On-Chain

Publishing a claim writes the following to the PGPRegistry contract on Ethereum. It is public and permanent: anyone can read it from any Ethereum node, forever. A claim can be revoked, which marks it inactive, but nothing on a public blockchain can be deleted, by us or by anyone.

Data Where it comes from Notes
Your Ethereum address The wallet that publishes the claim The claim is published from the address it names
Your PGP public key Pasted by you Includes the name(s) on the key. By default Thurin.id leaves out any name that contains an email address or carries a note (a notation) holding one, and refuses a signature with your email written into it; you can choose to include your email, and that choice cannot be undone for that claim
Proof links Notations on your key URLs of accounts you have chosen to link (for example a GitHub repository, a domain, a Mastodon profile). Adding a proof is your choice; each one publicly ties that account to this identity
A signed statement Made by you with your PGP key The text "I control the Ethereum address: …", signed. It proves the key holder chose this address
Timestamps The blockchain When the claim was published, updated, or revoked
Records Set by you, if you choose Short texts on a claim, such as a security contact, a canary, or a release list. Clearing one takes it off the claim, but every value it ever had stays readable in the blockchain's history

Before you publish, the attest page shows you exactly which names, proofs, and other notations are about to go on-chain. Nothing about you goes public unless you put it there. Replacing the key on a claim later, say to remove a proof, doesn't erase the old key: it stays readable in the blockchain's history, like everything else published there.

Processed only in your browser

  • Attesting: your PGP private key is used on your own machine, in your own tools, to sign the statement. The private key never leaves your machine, and no Thurin page ever asks for it.
  • Looking up an identity: your browser reads the claim from the blockchain and checks the signature against the stored key. Proofs are checked against the platforms they point at only when you press Check proofs (or chose Always check in the footer). The results are computed on your device and not reported anywhere.

What we do not collect

  • Accounts, logins, or passwords: there are none
  • Private keys (PGP or Ethereum)
  • Names, dates of birth, government identifiers, photos, or biometrics
  • Email content or contacts
  • Analytics, telemetry, or usage data: we run none

Web Servers and Logs

Our sites are static pages served through a web server (nginx) we operate, which fetches them from copies pinned to IPFS. It keeps no access logs: no record of who visited, which pages, or with what browser. Only a request that fails can leave a line with its IP address in the server's error log, which is rotated away after two weeks. When it fetches a page from the IPFS gateway, it sends nothing about you (no IP address, no browser details, and not which identity you are looking at).

Two small services answer requests directly: the keyserver (keys.thurin.id), which serves PGP keys from the blockchain to tools like gpg, and the relay (relay.thurin.id), which publishes a claim for you when you choose to have Thurin Labs pay for it. Neither records IP addresses. The keyserver's log says only what kind of lookup it answered and whether it found a key, never what was looked up. The relay logs what it published, which is public on-chain anyway, and for a request it turns away only that it did so, not who asked. It limits how often one caller can use it by counting requests in memory under a salted hash, forgotten after an hour and discarded when the service restarts.

The share-card service draws link previews, and the card images people put in READMEs and on pages (thurinlabs.id and thurin.id show one). It reads the same public on-chain data your browser would, fetches profile images only from the sources listed below, caches the result for about an hour, and stores nothing else. When a social platform or GitHub's image proxy asks for an image, it sees that request, not yours; when a page shows a card image straight from thurin.id, your browser asks our server for it, the same as for any page.

Our ENS-hosted copies (thurinlabs.eth, id.thurinlabs.eth) are served by third-party IPFS gateways such as eth.limo, which keep their own logs under their own policies.

Third Parties Your Browser Talks To

Thurin Labs sends nothing to third parties, and our pages tell outside services nothing about which page asked (no referrer). Your browser contacts these services directly, and each sees that request in the ordinary way:

  • An Ethereum node to read claims. By default thurin.id and the library use PublicNode's free public endpoint (ethereum.publicnode.com), which sees your IP address and the identities you look up. You can choose your own node in the thurin.id footer; the choice is saved only in your browser. Any node works.
  • ENS, via the same node, to resolve names. Profile images load only from content-addressed storage (through the Filebase IPFS gateway, with Pinata's as a fallback, or arweave.net) or from euc.li, where the ENS app stores uploaded avatars. An image on a server the name's owner controls is not loaded, so that owner cannot see who views the page. A name that keeps its data on a server of its owner's choosing (an "offchain" name) is followed only when you type that name yourself, and that server then sees your IP and the name; names the page looks up on its own are never followed.
  • The platforms behind proofs, only when you press Check proofs (or chose Always check): GitHub's API, Codeberg's API, Cloudflare's DNS-over-HTTPS resolver, and the Mastodon server named in a proof, which the identity's owner picks and so may see who checks. Farcaster proofs are checked through a public Farcaster node run by Quilibrium (haatz.quilibrium.com), with no account or key. The command-line tool checks proofs when you ask it to look someone up; thurin status --no-proofs asks only the Ethereum node.
  • Links on identity pages to Etherscan, the ENS app, and EFP contact nothing until you click them.
  • Your wallet. If you connect a mobile wallet by QR code, the pairing goes through WalletConnect's relay. Nothing is sent to WalletConnect until you choose to connect.

We use no third-party analytics, advertising, or tracking services, and no data is shared with anyone for marketing.

Cookies and Browser Storage

Our sites set no cookies. Your browser keeps a few things locally: your theme preference; on thurin.id, the wallet library's settings, and the wallet and address you connected so it can reconnect; the Ethereum node you chose, and whether to always check proofs, if you chose those; and, while you use a link from the Thurin CLI, that link, kept only in that tab and dropped once used. All stay on your device and can be cleared at any time.

Your Choices and Rights

  • What is public is your choice. You decide which names, which email (if any), and which proofs go on-chain, and the page shows you before you publish.
  • Revocation. You can revoke a claim at any time from thurin.id/attest. Revoked claims are marked inactive and are no longer treated as your identity; the record of them remains on the blockchain.
  • Updating. You can replace the key stored on a claim, for example to remove a proof, without a new claim. The old key stays readable in the blockchain's history.
  • Deletion. Blockchain data cannot be deleted by us or by anyone, including an old key you replaced and a record you cleared. This is a property of public blockchains, and it is why the attest page asks you to choose carefully.
  • Access. Everything Thurin.id knows about an identity is on the identity page, readable by anyone, including you. There is nothing further to request.

Security

  • All signing and verification happens on your device
  • The PGPRegistry contract has no owner, no administrator, no fees, and no upgrade mechanism; its source is published and verified
  • The verification library (identity-kit), the sites, and the contract are open source
  • No Thurin Labs system holds any secret of yours

Children's Privacy

Thurin.id is not directed at children. Publishing a claim requires an Ethereum wallet and a transaction fee.
