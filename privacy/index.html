Overview

Thurin Labs LLC ("Thurin Labs," "we," "our," or "us") builds Thurin.id (thurin.id), identity infrastructure for Ethereum. Thurin.id is an identity explorer and an attestation flow (thurin.id/attest) that binds an Ethereum address to a PGP key and to accounts on other platforms. This policy covers thurin.id, thurinlabs.id, docs.thurin.id, mathom.thurinlabs.id, the keyserver at keys.thurin.id, the relay at relay.thurin.id, and the copies of those sites served under our ENS names.

Thurin has no accounts, no database of users, and no server that reads or verifies anything on your behalf. Everything you publish goes on the Ethereum blockchain from your own wallet, and every check runs in your own browser. The only Thurin systems that see any traffic at all are the web servers, keyserver, and relay described below.

What Goes On-Chain

Publishing a claim writes the following to the PGPRegistry contract on Ethereum. It is public and permanent: anyone can read it from any Ethereum node, forever. A claim can be revoked, which marks it inactive, but nothing on a public blockchain can be deleted, by us or by anyone.

Data Where it comes from Notes
Your Ethereum address The wallet that publishes the claim The claim is published from the address it names
Your PGP public key Pasted by you Includes the name(s) on the key. By default Thurin leaves out any name that contains an email address; you can choose to include it, and that choice cannot be undone for that claim
Proof links Notations on your key URLs of accounts you have chosen to link (for example a GitHub repository, a domain, a Mastodon profile). Adding a proof is your choice; each one publicly ties that account to this identity
A signed statement Made by you with your PGP key The text "I control the Ethereum address: …", signed. It proves the key holder chose this address
Timestamps The blockchain When the claim was published, updated, or revoked

Before you publish, the attestation page shows you exactly which names and proofs are about to go on-chain. Nothing about you goes public unless you put it there.

Processed only in your browser

  • Attesting: your PGP private key is used on your own machine, in your own tools, to sign the statement. The private key never leaves your machine, and no Thurin page ever asks for it.
  • Looking up an identity: your browser reads the claim from the blockchain, checks the signature against the stored key, and checks each proof against the platform it points at. The results are computed on your device and not reported anywhere.

What we do not collect

  • Accounts, logins, or passwords: there are none
  • Private keys (PGP or Ethereum)
  • Names, dates of birth, government identifiers, photos, or biometrics
  • Email content or contacts
  • Analytics, telemetry, or usage data: we run none

Web Servers and Logs

Our sites are static pages served through a web server (nginx) we operate, which fetches them from copies pinned to IPFS. It keeps no access logs: no record of who visited, which pages, or with what browser. Only a request that fails can leave a line with its IP address in the server's error log, which is rotated away after two weeks. When it fetches a page from the IPFS gateway, it sends nothing about you (no IP address, no browser details, and not which identity you are looking at).

Two small services answer requests directly: the keyserver (keys.thurin.id), which serves PGP keys from the blockchain to tools like gpg, and the relay (relay.thurin.id), which publishes a claim for you when you choose to have Thurin pay for it. Neither records IP addresses. The relay limits how often one caller can use it by counting requests in memory under a salted hash, which is discarded when the service restarts; what it publishes is public on-chain anyway.

One small service of ours does more than serve files: the share-card service renders link previews and identity-card images when a social platform or another site requests one. It reads the same public on-chain data your browser would, caches the result for about an hour, and stores nothing else. It sees the requesting platform's request, not yours.

Our ENS-hosted copies (thurinlabs.eth, id.thurinlabs.eth) are served by third-party IPFS gateways such as eth.limo, which keep their own logs under their own policies.

Third Parties Your Browser Talks To

Thurin sends nothing to third parties. Your browser, however, contacts several services directly in order to verify an identity, and each of them sees that request in the ordinary way:

  • An Ethereum node to read claims. By default thurin.id, the library, and the embed use PublicNode's free public endpoint (ethereum.publicnode.com), which sees your IP address and the identities you look up. You can choose your own node in the thurin.id footer; the choice is saved only in your browser. Any node works.
  • ENS, via the same node, to resolve names. Profile images load only from content-addressed storage (through the ipfs.io or arweave.net gateway) or from euc.li, where the ENS app stores uploaded avatars. An image on a server the name's owner controls is not loaded, so that owner cannot see who views the page.
  • The platforms behind proofs: GitHub's API, Codeberg's API, Cloudflare's DNS-over-HTTPS resolver, and the Mastodon instance named in a proof. Farcaster proofs are checked through Neynar's API.
  • EFP (Ethereum Follow Protocol) for follower counts on identity pages.
  • Your wallet. If you connect a mobile wallet by QR code, the pairing goes through WalletConnect's relay. Nothing is sent to WalletConnect until you choose to connect.

We use no third-party analytics, advertising, or tracking services, and no data is shared with anyone for marketing.

Cookies and Browser Storage

Our sites set no cookies. Your browser keeps up to three things locally: your theme preference; on thurin.id, the wallet library's record of a connected wallet so it can reconnect; and the Ethereum node you chose, if you chose one. All stay on your device and can be cleared at any time.

Your Choices and Rights

  • What is public is your choice. You decide which names, which email (if any), and which proofs go on-chain, and the page shows you before you publish.
  • Revocation. You can revoke a claim at any time from thurin.id/attest. Revoked claims are marked inactive and are no longer treated as your identity; the record of them remains on the blockchain.
  • Updating. You can replace the key stored on a claim, for example to remove a proof, without a new claim.
  • Deletion. Blockchain data cannot be deleted by us or by anyone. This is a property of public blockchains, and it is why the attestation flow asks you to choose carefully.
  • Access. Everything Thurin knows about an identity is on the identity page, readable by anyone, including you. There is nothing further to request.

Security

  • All signing and verification happens on your device
  • The PGPRegistry contract has no owner, no administrator, no fees, and no upgrade mechanism; its source is published and verified
  • The verification library (identity-kit), the sites, and the contract are open source
  • No Thurin system holds any secret of yours

Children's Privacy

Thurin is not directed at children. Publishing a claim requires an Ethereum wallet and a transaction fee.

Legal Basis (GDPR)

For users in the European Economic Area: the only processing carried out by Thurin Labs systems is the web-server logging described above, on the basis of our legitimate interest in operating the sites. Everything else, including every claim and proof, is published by you, from your own wallet, to a public blockchain, by your own choice, and is processed on your device. Thurin Labs is not the controller of data you choose to publish on a public blockchain.

We do not collect your data because the design never needs it. What you publish is yours, public, and permanent, and the page tells you so before you do.
